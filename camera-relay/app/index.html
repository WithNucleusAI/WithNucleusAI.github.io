Camera Bridge

Jump to a step

A gentle setup, one step at a time

Be in the meeting, no matter where you are.

Some meeting websites only let people join from one country. This helper walks you through joining anyway — with your own face and voice — in small, simple steps. No tech background needed. Take it one screen at a time.

Easiest option first: if the country block seems like a mistake, ask whoever runs the meeting to turn it off. That's one click for them and skips everything here.
The idea in 30 seconds

You send a stand-in — wearing your face.

Think of a doorman who only lets in locals. Here's the trick:

1
A laptop that's already in the country joins for you
The website sees a local, so it lets it in.
2
We stream your camera over to that laptop
A small "courier" in the middle carries your picture and voice.
3
The laptop wears your face & voice
So the meeting sees and hears you — not the laptop.

You sit at your own computer the whole time and control that laptop from afar, like using someone else's screen over the internet.

Before we start

Four things you'll need.

About 20 minutes to set up once. After that, starting a call takes two minutes.

A laptop in the country
A Mac in the place you need to appear from — a friend's or home machine on normal home internet.
A remote-control app
Like Chrome Remote Desktop or AnyDesk, so you can see and use that laptop from where you are.
A small helper server
A tiny rented computer (about $5/month) that carries the video. A tech-savvy friend can set this up once — or you can, with our steps.
Two free apps on the laptop
Called OBS and BlackHole — we link you straight to them when it's time.
